Family Cruise Planner

A shared planner that lets a 12-person, multi-family group plan a milestone Alaska cruise without a travel agent or a group text. Everyone sees the same information, so each family can make its own decisions and own its own trip.

Problem

Planning a cruise for a big group usually means one person coordinates everything: collecting choices, relaying prices and answering the same questions in a group text, or paying a travel agent to do it. Decisions get made on partial information.

Built

A shared web app for the whole trip: cruise line and sailing comparison, port guides with excursion prices, each traveler's cabin, flights and passport expiration, family budgets, a to-do list, a shared log of calls and emails, and a "when to book" calendar.

Result

Four family members plan in it today. The organizer runs the budget, cruise comparison, cabins and flights from it, while each family adds its own excursions and tracks its own costs. No one has to coordinate anyone else's plans.

The full story

The problem, what I built and why, and what's next.

Why I built it

My family is planning a once-in-a-lifetime cruise for 12 people across several households. The usual options were a travel agent or one person coordinating everyone by text. I wanted every family to see the same facts, make their own choices and stop routing every question through the organizer.

What it does

  • Cruises & Ports: side-by-side comparison of cruise lines and sailings, a shortlist, port guides and researched excursions with typical prices.
  • Travelers: each person's cabin assignment, excursions and flights, plus a passport check that flags any passport that expires within six months of the trip. It stores expiration dates only, never passport numbers.
  • Budget & Prices: quotes, booked and paid amounts against a group budget, with each family's excursion costs tracked separately.
  • Questions & Log: notes from every call and email with the cruise line, airlines and hotels, so everyone hears the same answer and can decide for their own family.
  • Choices to Make, To-Dos and Calendar: open decisions with their status, a shared to-do list, and a timeline of when to book deposits, insurance, excursions and flights.

Decisions worth noting

  • Built for non-technical relatives: large readable type, plain-word menus and no jargon.
  • Families own their part: excursions and their costs belong to each family, so the shared budget stays clean.
  • Shared by default: every note, price and decision is visible to everyone, which is what replaced the group text.
