Detecting Insurance Fraud: Decision Tree vs. SVM

Binary classification on 1,000 insurance claims (67 features, 24.7% fraud). I trained and compared two models on a stratified 75/25 split and recommended one based on the cost of a missed fraud, not accuracy alone.

0.812SVM AUC (tree: 0.685)
67.7%SVM recall: 42 of 62 frauds caught
0.599Decision Tree F1, the better balance at default threshold
Question

Which model better flags fraudulent claims when a model that always says "not fraud" is already about 75% accurate?

Method

Orange Data Mining workflow: categorical target, stratified split, Decision Tree and SVM, then confusion matrices, ROC curves, precision, recall, F1 and AUC.

Finding

SVM, with an AUC of 0.812 vs. 0.685, ranks risky claims better and caught slightly more fraud. The tree is easier to explain to investigators.

The full story

The problem, what I built and why, and what's next.

Limitations I called out

  • Class imbalance pushes both models toward predicting "not fraud."
  • Both models used default settings; tuning tree depth or the SVM kernel and cost could help.
  • One 75/25 split; cross-validation would give a more reliable estimate.
  • Next models to try: Random Forest and Logistic Regression.
